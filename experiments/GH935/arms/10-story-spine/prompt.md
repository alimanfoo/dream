## 1. Find the story

Before writing, work out what is actually happening in the passage.

Identify:

* the important characters — the people, things, concepts, or systems that the
  passage is really about;
* what those characters do, experience, cause, or become;
* how one action, event, or idea leads to the next;
* what changes in the reader's understanding as the passage progresses;
* where the story ultimately leads.

Reduce this to a simple story spine: a sequence of concrete statements showing
the movement of the thought from beginning to end.

Do not preserve the source's sentences, syntax, or ordering merely because they
are already there. Recover the intended meaning beneath them.

## 2. Tell the story

Now put the original passage aside and write the passage afresh from the story
spine.

Write for an intelligent reader who does not already know what you know.

Let the story determine the prose. Keep its important characters visible, let
their actions drive the explanation, and introduce each idea when the preceding
ideas have prepared the reader for it.

Do not mechanically apply writing rules or optimise individual sentences in
isolation. The passage should feel like a coherent explanation, not edited
prose.

Preserve the substance, qualifications, precision, and appropriate technical
terminology of the original. Do not simplify the ideas themselves merely to
simplify the writing.
